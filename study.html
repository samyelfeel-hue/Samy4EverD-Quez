❤️ Medicinal Chemistry — CVS

CHAPTER 1 OF THE CVS STUDY GUIDE

Chapter 1 • Diuretics • study-guide preview based on the uploaded lecture

🧭 START HERE

🚀 Picture the kidney as a recycling factory

Blood enters the kidney, and the nephron decides which salts and water to recycle and which to let leave as urine. Diuretics change that recycling process at different stops.

💡 Everyday example

Imagine three checkpoints that normally return salt to the bloodstream. Blocking a checkpoint means more salt continues through the system, taking water along with it.

1. Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors

Representative drug: Acetazolamide

Site: Proximal convoluted tubule

Mechanism: Carbonic anhydrase inhibition decreases bicarbonate reabsorption, increasing sodium and water excretion.

Medicinal chemistry: Acetazolamide contains a sulfonamide group.

⭐ MUST MEMORIZE

Acetazolamide → carbonic anhydrase inhibitor → proximal tubule → relatively mild diuretic effect.

2. Thiazide Diuretics

🧠 Think of it like this

At the distal tubule checkpoint, the Na⁺/Cl⁻ transporter is a revolving door. Thiazides stop the door, so less sodium and chloride return to the blood.

Site: Distal convoluted tubule

Mechanism: Inhibit the Na⁺/Cl⁻ cotransporter.

DrugStudy point
ChlorothiazideThiazide diuretic
HydrochlorothiazideThiazide diuretic
IndapamideThiazide-like diuretic

Adverse effect: Hypokalemia; hypersensitivity is possible with sulfonamide-containing agents.

⭐ MUST MEMORIZE

Thiazides → DCT → Na⁺/Cl⁻ cotransporter → hypokalemia.

3. Loop Diuretics

💦 Visual memory

Picture the loop as the kidney’s powerful salt-recycling station. Furosemide blocks the Na⁺/K⁺/2Cl⁻ transporter there, producing a strong diuretic effect.

Representative drug: Furosemide (Lasix)

Site: Thick ascending limb of loop of Henle

Mechanism: Inhibit Na⁺/K⁺/2Cl⁻ cotransporter; produce a strong diuretic effect.

Lecture emphasis: Relatively rapid onset and short duration.

⭐ MUST MEMORIZE

Furosemide → loop diuretic → strong diuresis.

🎯 One-sentence summary

These diuretics work at different nephron sites, but all reduce salt reabsorption and thereby increase urine production.

🎮 Test your brain — Rapid Review

Which nephron segment does acetazolamide act on?

Proximal convoluted tubule.

What transporter do thiazides inhibit?

Na⁺/Cl⁻ cotransporter in the distal convoluted tubule.

What distinguishes loop diuretics?

Strong diuretic effect from action in the thick ascending limb.

Example: A drug blocks the Na⁺/Cl⁻ cotransporter. Which class?

Thiazide diuretic. The Na⁺/Cl⁻ cotransporter is the key clue.

Example: Which drug should you associate with a strong loop diuretic effect?

Furosemide. Remember: loop = powerful salt-recycling checkpoint.

🏁 FINISH LINE

Conclusion — what you should know now

You should be able to identify where each diuretic acts, name a representative drug, and connect its mechanism to increased urine production.
